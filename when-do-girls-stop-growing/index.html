When do girls stop growing?

Most girls are close to their adult height by 15 or 16. The first period is a better clock than age.

What the growth chart shows

The table shows the middle height for girls on the CDC chart. The last column shows how much that middle height rose in the year before.

The fastest year on the chart is 11 to 12, at about 7.2 cm (2.8 in). After 15, the middle height rises by less than 1 cm a year.

The chart is an average of many girls. One girl’s own spurt is sharper than the average and can come earlier or later.

The first period is the better clock

Puberty in girls usually starts between 8 and 13. The growth spurt comes early in puberty. The first period comes after the fastest growth.

  • Girls grow about 6.6 cm (2.6 in) on average after the first period.
  • About 8 cm (3.2 in) if it came before 11.
  • About 4.4 cm (1.7 in) if it came after 13.
  • Growth is essentially done about 4 years after it.

Use the growth after first period calculator for your own dates.

Signs that growth is ending

  • It is two or more years since the first period.
  • Height has changed by less than 1 cm over a full year.

What helps, and what does not

Adult height is mostly set by genes. Good habits support normal growth and health. They do not add extra height.

  • Sleep. Teens need 8 to 10 hours a night. Children 6 to 12 need 9 to 12 hours.
  • Movement. The World Health Organization advises an average of 60 minutes a day of activity for ages 5 to 17.
  • Food. Regular, varied meals with enough energy and protein. No supplement is needed for height.
  • Posture. Standing straight shows the height you already have. It does not change your bones.

Hanging and stretching do not make bones longer. They can add a few millimetres for less than an hour, because the spine decompresses. Then it goes back. Pills and powders sold for height do not work.

When to see a pediatrician

This site is not medical advice. These are screening signs doctors use. Any one of them is a good reason to book a visit and bring your height records.

  • Height is below the 3rd percentile for age (about 2 standard deviations below average).
  • Growth is slower than 5 cm (2 in) a year between age 5 and the start of puberty.
  • The projected adult height is more than 10 cm (4 in) below the mid-parental target.
  • No signs of puberty by 13 in girls or 14 in boys.
  • Signs of puberty before 8 in girls or before 9 in boys.

Most children with one of these signs are healthy. A doctor can check, and a hand X-ray for bone age tells more than any calculator.

Questions and answers

At what age do girls stop growing?
Most girls are close to adult height by 15 or 16. On the CDC chart the median girl gains less than 1 cm a year after 15. The better guide is the first period: growth is essentially done about 4 years after it.
Do girls grow after their first period?
Yes. About 6.6 cm on average. It is about 8 cm if the first period came before 11, and about 4.4 cm if it came after 13.
Do girls grow after 14?
Usually a little. The CDC median for girls is 160.4 cm (5 ft 3.1 in) at 14 and 163.3 cm (5 ft 4.3 in) at 20. That is 2.9 cm (1.2 in) more. A girl whose first period came late can gain more.
When is the growth spurt in girls?
On the CDC chart the median girl grows fastest between 11 and 12. The fastest growth comes before the first period, not after.
Can a girl still grow after growth has stopped?
No. Once the growth plates have closed, a girl will not grow taller. Supplements and stretching do not change that.
