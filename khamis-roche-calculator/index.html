The Khamis-Roche height prediction method

The best-known way to predict adult height without an X-ray. Here is what goes in, how far off it can be, and who it was built on.

Where to get a Khamis-Roche estimate

This page explains the method. It does not run it. The personal Khamis-Roche estimate is the paid result in the Beanstalk app.

Coming soon to iPhone and Android

Free on this site: the height predictor with a percentile projection, and the mid-parental height calculator.

What goes in

  • Sex
  • Age, from 4 to 17.5 years
  • Height today
  • Weight today
  • The average of the mother’s and father’s heights

How it works

The method is a weighted sum. Each input is multiplied by a number, and the results are added up.

The numbers change with age and sex. There is one set for every half year from 4 to 17.5. At young ages the parents’ heights count for more. In the late teens, the child’s own height counts for almost everything.

The original paper works in inches and pounds.

How far off it can be

The published 90% error is about 5.3 cm (2.1 in) for boys and 4.3 cm (1.7 in) for girls.

That means 9 in 10 children in the study ended up within that distance of the prediction. One in 10 ended up further away. A prediction of 175 cm for a boy really means “probably between about 170 and 180 cm”.

The paper also gives errors by age. We have not read those into the app yet. Until then the app widens the range by a fixed 25% in the usual puberty years. That figure is our own choice.

Limits

  • The study group. The method was built on white US children in the Fels Longitudinal Study. It may be less accurate for children of other backgrounds.
  • Early and late developers. The method does not know when puberty started. It can predict too high for early developers and too low for late developers.
  • Health. It assumes a healthy child. It is not meant for children with a condition or treatment that affects growth.
  • Parents’ heights. Remembered heights add error. Measured heights are better.
  • A bone-age X-ray is more accurate. If a precise answer matters, ask a pediatrician.

How the Beanstalk app uses it

The app runs Khamis-Roche for ages 4 to 17.5 when it has weight and both parents’ heights. It always shows the 90% range, not just one number. It widens the range during puberty and when parents’ heights are from memory.

We have not yet checked every number in our table against the printed paper and its 1995 correction. That check will be finished before the app is released. See the methodology for the details.

Questions and answers

What is the Khamis-Roche method?
It is a way to predict adult height without an X-ray. It uses the child’s age, sex, height and weight, and the average of the parents’ heights. Harry Khamis and Alex Roche published it in 1994.
How accurate is the Khamis-Roche method?
The published 90% error is about 5.3 cm for boys and 4.3 cm for girls. So about 9 in 10 children in the study ended up within that distance of their prediction. About 1 in 10 were further off.
What ages does it cover?
Ages 4 to 17.5. It is not meant for children under 4.
Who was it built on?
White children from Ohio in the Fels Longitudinal Study. It has not been tested as well in other groups, and it may be less accurate for them.
Is a bone-age X-ray better?
Yes. A hand and wrist X-ray shows how mature the bones are. Methods that use bone age are more accurate, most of all for early and late developers. Only a doctor can order one.
Is there a Khamis-Roche calculator on this page?
No. The Khamis-Roche estimate is the paid result inside the Beanstalk app. The free tools on this site give the height percentile, a percentile projection and the mid-parental range.
