Methodology

Every method we use, every number in it, and where the number comes from. We also list what we assumed and what we have not yet verified.

Last checked: 2026-11-12. The website and the app share one calculation engine, so the numbers below are read straight from its code.

Which method is used when

  • On this website: the CDC percentile, the percentile projection, the mid-parental range, and growth after the first period.
  • In the app, ages 4 to 17.5, with weight and both parents’ heights: Khamis-Roche.
  • In the app, ages 2 to 20, without those: the percentile projection.
  • In the app, under 2: the mid-parental target only.
  • 18 and over: treated as at adult height.

In the app, puberty widens the range. It never moves the estimate. A first period caps the top of the range. No estimate is ever below today’s height.

CDC height percentile

We use the CDC 2000 stature-for-age table for ages 2 to 20. For each month of age it gives L, M and S. We take a straight line between the months.

The z-score is ((height ÷ M)L − 1) ÷ (L × S). The percentile is the normal curve value for that z-score. We compute the normal curve with a standard formula that is accurate to better than 0.00002 percentile points.

Limit: the chart was built from US children measured between 1963 and 1994.

Percentile projection

We take today’s z-score and read off the height with the same z-score at age 20.

The 90% range is 1.645 × 5 cm = 8.2 cm either side. The 5 cm is the upper end of the 4 to 5 cm standard error reported by Cole and Wright for ages 4 to puberty. Outside those ages the true error is likely larger.

Assumed: keeping the same z-score. In real data, children drift a little towards the average. We plan to replace this with the Cole and Wright correlations.

Assumed: the range is multiplied by 1.25 for boys aged 12 to 16 and girls aged 10 to 14.

Mid-parental range (Tanner)

Target = (mother + father + 13 cm) ÷ 2 for a boy, and (mother + father − 13 cm) ÷ 2 for a girl. The range is 8.5 cm either side.

On this website the heights are used as typed. In the app, a height marked “from memory” is corrected first, because people tend to report themselves a little taller than they measure.

Khamis-Roche (app only)

Predicted adult height = intercept + a × height + b × weight + c × mid-parent height, in inches and pounds. The four numbers change every half year from age 4 to 17.5, and differ for boys and girls.

90% range: 5.3 cm either side for boys and 4.3 cm for girls.

Not yet verified. Our table of Khamis-Roche numbers has not been read against the printed 1994 paper and its 1995 correction. Here is what we know:

  • The intercept, height and mid-parent columns agree between two open copies of the table.
  • The weight column was on two different scales in one of those copies. We use the scale that gives a sensible result for a median child.
  • One cell differs between the copies in the last digit.
  • The 90% errors come from secondary reports. The by-age errors in the paper are not yet read in.

Every cell will be checked against the paper before the app is released.

Limits: the method was built on white US children in the Fels Longitudinal Study. It does not use bone age. A bone-age X-ray is more accurate. More about the method.

Growth after the first period

We use the averages in Gaete 2025 by age at first period: before 11, after 13, and in between. The top of the range is the average plus 1.645 standard deviations.

Assumed: after the first year, the remaining growth is spread evenly until 4 years after the first period.

Adults

From 18, we show today’s height as adult height. For men under 21 the app leaves room for late growth, based on the 2.13 cm average gain between 18 and 27 in Hulanicka and Kotlarz 1983.

Growth rate (app only)

The app works out centimetres per year from two measurements at least 6 months apart. Shorter gaps are too noisy to mean anything.

Screening signs

These limits decide when the tools suggest seeing a pediatrician. They are prompts, not a diagnosis.

  • Height more than 2 standard deviations below average (below about the 3rd percentile).
  • Growth under 5 cm a year from age 5 to puberty.
  • Projected adult height more than 10 cm below the mid-parental target.
  • No puberty signs by 13 in girls or 14 in boys.
  • Puberty signs before 8 in girls or 9 in boys.

The first three follow Barstow and Rerucha 2015. The puberty ages follow the Pediatric Endocrine Society.

Habits

The app also covers everyday habits such as sleep, activity and meals. These support normal growth and health. We do not claim they add height. Sleep targets follow the American Academy of Sleep Medicine. Activity targets follow the World Health Organization.

Questions and answers

Which methods run on this website?
Three: the CDC height percentile, a projection that keeps the same percentile to age 20, and the Tanner mid-parental range. The growth-after-first-period calculator uses the Gaete 2025 averages.
Which method runs only in the app?
The Khamis-Roche prediction. It needs weight and both parents’ heights, and it is the paid result in the app.
Have all the numbers been verified?
No, and this page says which. The Khamis-Roche table has not yet been checked cell by cell against the printed paper and its 1995 correction. Some range settings are our own choices.
What does a 90% range mean?
If the method is right about its own error, 9 in 10 people end up inside the range and 1 in 10 end up outside it.
